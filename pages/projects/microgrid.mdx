# Energy & Microgrid Analysis

An analysis of how electricity demand, climate conditions, and renewable energy resources vary across U.S. regions, with a focus on identifying patterns relevant to microgrid planning.

## Overview

This project examined relationships between regional electricity demand and environmental conditions, combining energy, climate, solar, and wind data across the United States.

The goal was to identify groups of regions with similar combinations of:

* Electricity demand
* Temperature and seasonal conditions
* Solar power access
* Wind power access
* Energy vulnerability

## Methods

The analysis combined several statistical and machine learning techniques:

**Feature Engineering**
Monthly summaries for zip codes were scaled using appropriate scalars for each data type, then each zip code's features were fit to a sin wave, then the fitted sin wave's features such as phase, amplitude, average, and fit r2 were used for the following steps.

**Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**
Reduced the dimensionality of the feature space while preserving the dominant patterns in the data, explaining 95% of variance with 9 features instead of 20.


**Clustering**
Used K-Means clustering to identify regions with similar combinations of demand and environmental characteristics, from the 9 components detailed above.

**Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA)**
Visualized the 9-dimensional space using only two dimensions that roughly showed how zip codes related to one another. This offered an alternative visualization to the geographic projection that is also shown in the poster.

## Results

The analysis revealed distinct regional patterns driven by combinations of electricity demand and environmental conditions rather than by geography alone.

Some regions showed relatively unique combinations of demand and renewable-resource characteristics, while other regions formed broader groups with gradually changing climate and demand conditions.
